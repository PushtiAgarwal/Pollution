Pollution is the introduction of unwanted and harmful substances into the natural environment.

It occurs when natural processes are disrupted by external contaminants. Pollution alters the physical, chemical, or biological properties of the environment. It results in negative impacts on living organisms and natural ecosystems. Pollutants can be solid, liquid, gaseous, or even forms of energy. They originate from a variety of sources, both natural and human-made.

Natural events like volcanic eruptions can cause pollution, though human activities are the primary contributors. Pollution leads to the degradation of air, water, soil, and other natural resources. It creates imbalances that threaten the stability of ecological systems. Pollution can accumulate over time, intensifying its harmful effects. It may be localized or spread across vast geographical areas.

Some forms of pollution are visible, while others are invisible and harder to detect. It can have immediate as well as long-term impacts on health and the environment. Persistent pollution can permanently alter natural landscapes and biological communities. Thus, pollution represents a major disruption to the natural order of the environment.
